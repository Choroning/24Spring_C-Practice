## 2. Schedule and Class Format

### Computer Language Ⅰ (DCCS101-04)

- **Credits (Hours):** 3 (4)
- **Intended For:** First-year students
- **Class Times:** Monday periods 8-9, Tuesday periods 8-9
- **Classrooms:** Nongshim International Hall Room 308, Nongshim International Hall Room 207 (Computer Lab 1)
- **Course Code in the Syllabus:** `DCCS151-04`
- **Class Format:** Theory, labs, and quizzes, with lecture, lab, and quiz as the activity types in the syllabus. Because the course is theory-oriented, the syllabus asks students to take Computer Language and Training Ⅰ alongside it if possible. The specific goals of each type are as follows.
  - Lecture: Centered on the textbook, lectures first build a sufficient understanding of concepts such as the basic concepts of programming languages, the way data is represented in a computer, and the operators and control statements of C, and then look at usage through grammar explanations and examples.
  - Lab: The lab sessions, in which students apply the new concepts learned in the theory lectures to solve given lab problems, are a very important part of the course. By grasping the given problem precisely, thinking about how to solve it, and expressing the chosen solution in logic that follows the programming grammar, students train the basic ability of a software developer called "problem solving using software".
  - Quiz: Quizzes are used to understand newly learned concepts precisely. Solving problems on basic concepts and topics that learners may find confusing raises the understanding of the class, and encouraging students to take part in going over the quizzes is expected to help presenters organize their thoughts and to develop the presentation skills of conveying one's thoughts to others and persuading them.
- **Attendance:** Attendance is checked (the course is not exempt from attendance checks).
- **Exam Supervision:** Exams are not held unsupervised.
- **Class Description:** Students acquire the basic theory and skills of computer programming through C, a structured programming language.
- **Study Objectives:**
  1. Understand programming languages.
  2. Acquire basic knowledge of the C language.
  3. Practice logic design for problem solving.
  4. Solve problems through C programming.
- **Course and Departmental Objectives:** The most basic element of software development is programming ability. By learning C, which can be called the foundational language of programming, the course builds the basics of programming and of problem solving with software, and provides the basic knowledge for acquiring new applied technologies.
- **Prerequisites:** The syllabus designates no recommended prerequisites or enrollment requirements.

### Computer Language and Training Ⅰ (DCCS103-03)

- **Credits (Hours):** 1 (2)
- **Intended For:** First-year students
- **Class Times:** Monday periods 6-7 (starting at 2 p.m.)
- **Classroom:** Nongshim International Hall Room 209
- **Course Code in the Syllabus:** `DCCS103 (03)`, while the Week 1 course plan material lists it as `DCCS153 (02, 03)`.
- **Class Format:** The activity types in the syllabus are lecture, lab, and quiz. Students learn the detailed theory of the computer language in class, and some topics are practiced in labs so that students understand the theory clearly. The syllabus planned solving and explaining the textbook lab problems every week, and the actual classes went through the solutions to the previous assignment, textbook theory, and practice with textbook problems in that order.
- **Class Mode:** The first semester of 2024 is taught in person, and the midterm and final exams are in principle held in person. When students have difficulty attending because of COVID-19 or a class falls on a public holiday, videos recorded in 2021 or newly recorded in 2023 are provided as lecture material instead of makeup classes.
- **COVID-19 Provisions:** The syllabus states that classes are in person in principle but may be held as real-time hybrid online and offline classes (NeMO Class) or as real-time online lectures, that classes are fully online in principle under level 3 social distancing, and that online classes are given as online or recorded lectures.
- **Attendance:** Attendance is checked (the course is not exempt from attendance checks).
- **Exam Supervision:** Exams are not held unsupervised.
- **Linked Course:** The syllabus links this course to DCCS101 Computer Language Ⅰ.
- **Course Management:** Course announcements, course materials, and assignment submissions go through Blackboard.
- **Class Description:** Solving problems with a computer requires the process called programming, and the most basic of these programs can be said to be C. The course teaches students to know and understand the basic concepts of C precisely, and improves their C programming skills through practice with a variety of examples so that they can use it extensively in practice.
- **Study Objectives:**
  1. Understand programming.
  2. Acquire knowledge of the C language.
  3. Solve problems through C programming.
- **Course and Departmental Objectives:** The syllabus relates the course to the following abilities.
  1. The ability to apply theory and knowledge acquired in mathematics, basic science, and general education to the major
  2. The ability to recognize and model problems related to computer and information technology
  3. The ability to play a role as a member of a multidisciplinary team
- **Design Objectives:**
  1. Understand the given problem.
  2. Analyze the requirements.
  3. Develop an algorithm.
  4. Write pseudocode (Pseudo Code).
  5. Choose the tool to use and organize the necessary elements. Use the given tool (MS Visual Studio).
  6. Code and debug in C. Include the header files defined for using various classes, and develop programs systematically.
  7. Performance improvement: Find errors by comparing against the grammatical and logical errors learned in class.
- **Prerequisites:** The syllabus designates no recommended prerequisites or enrollment requirements.
- **Lab Environment:** Visual Studio Community 2022 is used. The Week 1 materials guide downloading and installing it and creating a C project, writing code, and running it, the additional Week 2 material covers how to run code when a project has several C files with a `main` function, and the Week 11 material covers how to set up pointer addresses to print in 32 bits.

| Item | Weight |
|:-----|-----:|

| Final Exam | 40% |
| Assignments | 10% |

- The syllabus marks assessment scores as not disclosed, but it states that the midterm, final exam, and assignment scores are disclosed to the whole class or individually.
- The midterm and final exams test whether students understand C as a whole, and each is taken out of 100 points and then converted to 40 points for grading.
- When a chapter ends, the textbook exercises are given as an assignment. The assignments consist of the exercises of each chapter and programming lab assignments, and Assignments 1-10 were given.
- An absence deducts 1 point, and a late arrival deducts 0.5 points.
- A student who misses the midterm or final exam, behaves in a way unbecoming of a student, scores below 40 on the 100-point scale, or is absent for one third or more of all class days receives an F.
- Students must submit four PL Help Center consultation certificates, two each during the midterm and final exam periods, and each missing submission deducts 2 points.
- A student with poor class conduct receives one warning, and if the conduct does not improve afterwards, points are deducted or no grade is given depending on the situation.
- The syllabus states that the grading criteria or the points of each criterion may change because of COVID-19. Exams are in person in principle, may be replaced by remote exams or assignments depending on the situation, and the exam period is extended to two weeks for in-person exams.
- **Assignment Submission:** Students write their answers in the assignment submission template (.hwp) distributed by the course and submit them only on Blackboard. Coding problems must include the code, a code analysis, and a screenshot of the execution result, and the template must have a photo that identifies the student. The deadline is before class (2 p.m.) on the following Monday, and after the deadline the system automatically hides the assignment.
- **Exam Format:** The midterm and final exams are lab exams in which students write code on a computer. Students enter at least 10 minutes before the exam starts, create a folder named `A_StudentID_Name_Section` or `B_StudentID_Name_Section`, submit the answers on a USB drive, and leave the exam paper on the desk without turning off the computer. A student who does not hand in the exam paper receives an F. An answer that does not run receives 0 points, and even for problems with partial credit, the relevant part must run to earn points.
- **Exam Schedule:** The midterm exam was held at 2 p.m. on 2024-04-22 (Mon) in the classroom and covered the material up to week 7, and the final exam was held at 2 p.m. on 2024-06-17 (Mon) in Nongshim Hall Room 209.

## 4. Course Policies

### Computer Language Ⅰ (DCCS101-04)

- For smooth progress and understanding, students must read the textbook before attending each class and take an active part in class by asking questions and so on.
- **Support for Students with Disabilities:** Students with disabilities may be supported by helpers selected from among the enrolled students for effective learning.
- **Academic Ethics:** Students who take part in the university's educational activities have the following general duties in every part of completing the curriculum, including attending lectures, preparing and submitting assignments, and taking exams.
  1. Take an active part in all learning with a sincere and honest mind and a passionate attitude in accordance with the purpose of the university and its educational philosophy.
  2. Be aware of one's position as a cultured person receiving higher education, and devote oneself to creative academic inquiry based on moral sensitivity.
  3. Faithfully keep the promises made to faculty or fellow students in connection with educational activities.
  4. Respect the personality of faculty and fellow students and observe basic courtesy.
- If credits are found to have been obtained through dishonest acts, such as plagiarizing assignments, reusing assignments from other classes, or cheating on exams, or through other improper means, the credits are canceled even if they have already been granted, and disciplinary actions such as reprimand, suspension, expulsion, or dismissal may be taken in consideration of the circumstances.
- The university operates a plagiarism detection system (SafeAssign on Blackboard) to raise awareness of academic ethics and to encourage creativity, and students must check in advance whether an assignment constitutes plagiarism when submitting it.

### Computer Language and Training Ⅰ (DCCS103-03)

- **Classroom Rules:** The Week 1 course plan material gives the following rules.
  1. During class, students do not use the internet or KakaoTalk for anything unrelated to the class, and do so during breaks or after class.
  2. A student who urgently needs to make a call or use KakaoTalk raises a hand, gets the professor's confirmation, leaves quietly to deal with it, and comes back right away. Students do not leave in the middle of class without saying anything.
  3. A student who feels sick raises a hand to let the professor know and then goes to a hospital or the like.
  4. A student who urgently needs the restroom raises a hand so that the professor can see it, makes eye contact, and goes quietly.
  5. Students do not behave in ways that go beyond minimal courtesy.
- **Writing Code:** Students always write code on their own and ask about what they do not understand. Questions about an assignment, however, are asked after its submission period has ended.
- **Inquiries:** Unless urgent, inquiry emails are sent on weekdays between 9 a.m. and 6 p.m., must state the student ID, name, department, course name, and section, and must state the point of the question clearly and politely. A question identifies precisely the content or code the student does not understand (e.g., why B comes out when the code was written expecting A), and the student first thinks the problem through before asking.
- **Support for Students with Disabilities:** Students with disabilities may be provided with helpers for effective learning.
- **Academic Ethics:** The syllabus provisions on students' academic ethics duties, the cancellation of credits and disciplinary actions for dishonest acts, and the plagiarism detection system (SafeAssign) are the same as in Computer Language Ⅰ.
